The Idea: Could Positioning Reveal Market Turning Points?

I spent two days building a Power BI dashboard to explore a question: could changes in speculative futures positioning help identify potential tops and bottoms in gold prices?

The dashboard brings together CFTC Commitments of Traders (COT) data and weekly closing prices for Gold, Silver, Bitcoin, and Crude Oil.

Analytical Approach

  • Prepared historical CFTC positioning data for four markets.
  • Combined weekly positioning observations with weekly closing-price data for exploratory comparison.
  • Built interactive Power BI visuals for Long, Short, Net Position, and week-over-week changes.
  • Compared speculative positioning with gold price behavior and explored Pearson correlation.
  • Evaluated whether the observed relationships supported the original market-timing hypothesis.

Timing note: COT positions are reported as of Tuesday, while the price series used Friday closes. These are different observation dates; a same-week comparison should not be interpreted as a validated predictive test.

The Disappointing Discovery

I expected to uncover a useful signal. Instead, the relationship between the positioning measures I tested and gold's weekly closing prices appeared weak—and at times inverse.

Even after exploring Pearson correlation, I did not find a dependable standalone signal for calling gold market tops or bottoms in the dataset examined.

Two days of work, and no reliable market-timing signal. It was disappointing, but it was also an important analytical result.

What the Result Does—and Doesn't—Mean

The analysis did not support my initial hypothesis within the measures and period examined. It does not establish that COT data has no value, or that no relationship could emerge using different lags, transformations, or market conditions.

Correlation alone does not demonstrate predictive power. A stronger test would account for release timing, potential lags, out-of-sample evaluation, and alternative measures such as changes in positioning rather than levels.

What I Learned

Not every dataset confirms the story we hope to find. Good analysis means testing an idea, questioning the evidence, and reporting the result—even when it is disappointing.

This project strengthened my skills in data preparation, data modeling, DAX, interactive dashboard design, and analytical reasoning.
